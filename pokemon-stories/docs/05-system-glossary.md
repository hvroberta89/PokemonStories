# System Glossary

## Purpose

This glossary defines the official terminology used throughout the Pokémon Stories project.

It establishes a shared ubiquitous language for developers, designers, writers and AI assistants.

Every term in this document has a single, precise meaning.

The same concept should never have multiple names.

Likewise, the same name should never refer to different concepts.

When introducing new features, always reuse existing terminology before creating new terms.

---

# Core Concepts

## Project

A Project represents an entire campaign world.

It is the highest-level container in the application.

A Project contains everything required to run a long-term campaign, including adventures, characters, locations, world facts and session history.

A Project is expected to live for months or even years.

---

## Adventure

An Adventure is a playable story within a Project.

It represents a single quest, mission or narrative episode.

An Adventure is designed before gameplay and played during one or more Sessions.

Examples:

- The Lost Pokémon Egg
- The Mist Forest
- The Secret Laboratory

---

## Session

A Session is a single live gameplay event.

A Session begins when players start playing an Adventure and ends when gameplay finishes.

Sessions produce notes, rewards and world changes.

Multiple Sessions may belong to the same Adventure.

---

## Scene

A Scene is the smallest playable narrative unit.

A Scene answers four questions:

- Where are the players?
- What is happening?
- Who is present?
- What is the immediate objective?

Scenes are played sequentially but are not required to follow a fixed path.

---

## Story

A Story is the complete narrative experienced by the players.

Stories emerge through gameplay.

Stories are not stored as predefined scripts.

Instead, they are created collaboratively by the Game Master and the players.

---

# World

## Character

A Character represents a player-controlled trainer.

Characters persist across Adventures.

They own progression, Pokémon, inventory and achievements.

---

## Pokémon

A Pokémon is a collectible and story participant owned by a Character.

The application focuses on narrative progression rather than competitive mechanics.

Examples of progression:

- Seen
- Encountered
- Caught
- Trained
- Evolved

---

## NPC

A Non-Player Character is controlled by the Game Master.

NPCs belong to the Project and may appear in multiple Adventures.

---

## Location

A Location is a reusable place in the campaign world.

Locations are shared between Adventures.

Examples:

- Viridian Forest
- Pewter City
- Hidden Cave

---

## World Fact

A World Fact represents permanent knowledge about the campaign world.

Examples:

- The bridge has been repaired.
- Team Rocket controls the laboratory.
- Professor Oak trusts the players.

World Facts persist across Adventures.

---

# Gameplay

## Encounter

An Encounter is an interactive challenge.

Examples include:

- Wild Pokémon
- Conversation
- Puzzle
- Battle
- Environmental challenge

Encounters always belong to a Scene.

---

## Goal

A Goal describes the current objective of the players.

Only one primary Goal should be active at a time during live gameplay.

Examples:

- Find the missing egg.
- Escape the cave.
- Reach the laboratory.

---

## Timeline

The Timeline records important events during a Session.

Timeline entries are ordered chronologically.

They later become the basis of the Session Summary.

---

## Session Summary

The Session Summary is a narrative recap generated after gameplay.

It summarizes:

- major events
- player decisions
- rewards
- discoveries
- world changes

The Summary is reviewed before updating the Project.

---

# AI Concepts

## AI Suggestion

An AI Suggestion is a proposal generated by artificial intelligence.

Suggestions never become canonical automatically.

They require approval by the Game Master.

---

## Narrative Context

Narrative Context is the curated information sent to the AI.

It may contain:

- Adventure
- Characters
- NPCs
- Locations
- World Facts
- Previous Session Summaries

It never includes the complete Project.

---

## Prompt

A Prompt is an instruction sent to the AI provider.

Prompts are infrastructure concerns.

They are not part of the domain model.

---

# Rewards

## Reward

A Reward is something earned through gameplay.

Examples:

- Pokémon
- Item
- Badge
- Outfit
- Achievement
- Printable sticker

Rewards exist independently of printing.

---

## Collection

The Collection represents all long-term player progress.

Examples:

- Pokédex
- Inventory
- Badges
- Achievements
- Cards

Collections persist throughout the Project.

---

## Printable Asset

A Printable Asset is a physical representation of a Reward.

Examples:

- Sticker
- Card
- Badge
- Certificate

Printing does not create ownership.

Printing only creates a physical artifact.

---

# State Definitions

## Idea

An Adventure exists only as a concept.

---

## Designing

The Adventure is actively being created.

---

## Ready

The Adventure contains all required information for gameplay.

Optional content may still be missing.

---

## Running

The Adventure currently has an active Session.

---

## Completed

The Adventure has been successfully played.

Future Sessions may still be created if desired.

---

## Archived

The Adventure is no longer actively used.

---

## Prepared

A Reward has been configured but not yet earned.

---

## Unlocked

The players have earned the Reward.

---

## Printed

A physical version has been produced.

---

## Given

The physical Reward has been handed to the player.

Printed and Given are intentionally separate states.

---

# User Roles

## Game Master

The primary user of the application.

Responsible for:

- creating Adventures
- running Sessions
- approving AI Suggestions
- updating the campaign world

The application is designed around the Game Master's workflow.

---

## Player

A participant in the Adventure.

Players interact with the story rather than the application.

Future versions may include optional player-facing functionality.

---

# Technology Terms

## Domain

Contains business rules and domain concepts.

Independent of frameworks and infrastructure.

---

## Application

Coordinates use cases and workflows.

---

## Infrastructure

Communicates with external systems.

Examples:

- AI providers
- Storage
- Printing
- Analytics

---

## Presentation

Responsible for the user interface.

Contains no business rules.

---

# Naming Rules

When writing code or documentation:

Always use:

- Project
- Adventure
- Session
- Scene
- Character
- NPC
- Location
- World Fact
- Reward
- Collection
- Narrative Context

Avoid introducing synonyms such as:

- Campaign (when Project is meant)
- Mission (when Adventure is meant)
- Chapter (when Scene is meant)
- Save Game
- Level
- Quest Log (unless intentionally different)

Consistency is more valuable than perfect wording.

---

# Final Principle

Language shapes architecture.

A consistent vocabulary creates a consistent system.
